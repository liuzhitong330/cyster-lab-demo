A spatial reading of how inflammation replaces a familiar chemokine route with a transcellular oxysterol signal at the lymph-node gateway.

By Cathy Liu Immunology · cell migration · research tools liuzhitong330@gmail.com

The question

The Cyster Lab studies how immune cells find the right tissue compartment and how those spatial decisions shape an immune response. A recent study asks a particularly useful version of that question: when inflammation suppresses the homeostatic CCL21 cue around high endothelial venules, what guides naive lymphocytes into the node?

I built this short interactive note to organize the evidence around two linked ideas: a context-dependent switch in recruitment requirements, and a multi-cell pathway that assembles the replacement signal. It is a structured interpretation of published results—not a reanalysis of the raw sequencing files.

Under homeostatic conditions, CCL21 surrounding the venule supports CCR7-dependent entry. After local viral inflammation, CCL21 falls while CCL19 is retained, and the oxysterol receptor EBI2 becomes much more important for B-cell recruitment. In the imaging analysis, wild-type B cells moved roughly twice as far from the venule as their EBI2-deficient counterparts.

The same receptor logic appears at a second inflamed boundary: EBI2-deficient B cells showed a substantial entry defect in MC38 tumors, whereas transferred T cells did not show the same significant homing defect. The result suggests a reusable inflammatory gateway program rather than a lymph-node-only exception.

The mechanism is spatially economical. Endothelial Ch25h produces 25-HC at the gateway; fibroblastic reticular-cell Cyp7b1 completes the ligand; and EBI2 lets an arriving B cell interpret the resulting cue. Langerhans cells provide an additional Ch25h source after skin inflammation. The gradient is therefore a tissue-level product, not the output of a single cell.

The accompanying GEO series, GSE281684, contains four mouse ATAC-seq samples from sorted CD45− CD31− gp38+ fibroblastic reticular cells, comparing inflamed popliteal nodes with non-draining brachial nodes 24 hours after footpad LCMV infection. That dataset is a useful starting point for asking which regulatory programs let stromal cells support this switch.

Why this matters

A practical next experiment

I would pair short-term competitive homing with flow-sorted stromal populations and spatial imaging across a time course. That would connect receptor dependence to when and where the tissue builds the ligand, while distinguishing recruitment from later retention or survival effects.

Methods and limits

What this note does—and does not—claim

This project is based on a structured reading of the published Cell study, its public supplementary record, and the metadata for GSE281684. I did not download or recompute the approximately 1.7 GB of raw ATAC-seq data. Route positions are illustrative; quantitative labels reproduce paper-reported comparisons.

The results come from murine LCMV inflammation and MC38 tumor models. The interactive comparison is intended to clarify experimental logic, not to extrapolate directly to human disease or imply causality beyond the cited genetic perturbations.
